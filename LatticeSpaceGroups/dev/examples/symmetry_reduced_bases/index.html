Symmetry-reduced bases

This is what the package is for. SymBasis.jl builds a basis that conserves a symmetry, and it needs that symmetry as a site permutation. Loading the two packages together activates an extension so the symmetry constructors take a lattice directly.

using LatticeSpaceGroups, SymBasis

Momentum sectors of a chain

Take a periodic chain of eight spin-1/2 sites.

nsites = 8
lat = build(Hypercube([nsites]; periodic=true))
dofo = dof_object(Spin(1 // 2))

full = length(basis(dofo, nsites).states)
256

Translational symmetry has one sector per momentum number k. Translational(k, lat) derives its generator from the lattice rather than making you write it:

dims = [length(basis(dofo, nsites, sym(Translational(k, lat), dofo)).states) for k in 0:(nsites-1)]
8-element Vector{Int64}:
 36
 30
 33
 30
 34
 30
 33
 30

The decisive check is that these partition the full space — every state belongs to exactly one momentum sector, so the dimensions must sum to 2^8. If the permutation were wrong, this sum would not come out:

sum(dims), full
(256, 256)

Composing symmetries

Symmetries compose with ∘, which is how you reach a small sector of a large space. Fixing the total magnetization to zero first:

sz = sym(TotalMagnetization(0 // 1, nsites), dofo)
length(basis(dofo, nsites, sz).states), binomial(nsites, nsites ÷ 2)
(70, 70)

and then splitting that by momentum:

sector_dims = [
    length(basis(dofo, nsites, sz ∘ sym(Translational(k, lat), dofo)).states)
    for k in 0:(nsites-1)
]
sum(sector_dims), binomial(nsites, nsites ÷ 2)
(70, 70)

The largest sector is a fraction of the full $2^8$-dimensional space, which is the entire point of the exercise:

maximum(sector_dims), full
(10, 256)

The same code in two dimensions

Nothing above was specific to a chain. On a 2 × 3 torus, axis selects which direction to translate along — and this is the case where writing the permutation by hand stops being realistic:

square = build(Hypercube([2, 3]; periodic=true))
n = n_sites(square)

translation_permutation(square, 2)
6-element Vector{Int64}:
 3
 4
 5
 6
 1
 2
dims2 = [
    length(basis(dofo, n, sym(Translational(k, square; axis=2), dofo)).states)
    for k in 0:2
]
sum(dims2), 2^n
(64, 64)

Reflections and rotations

SpatialReflection takes a parity, and the mirror plane is placed wherever the lattice actually admits one — not assumed to be at the origin:

open_chain = build(Hypercube([6]; periodic=false))
reflection_permutation(open_chain, 1)
6-element Vector{Int64}:
 6
 5
 4
 3
 2
 1

That is the reversal 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, the mirror sitting at the chain's midpoint rather than at site 1. See Boundary conditions and symmetry for why that matters.

For rotations, inspect what a lattice offers before asking for one — a chain has no non-trivial proper rotation about a point, and Rotational throws rather than silently giving you the identity:

length(rotation_permutations(build(Square(3; periodic=true))))
3
